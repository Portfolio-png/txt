Pipeline Editor Field Guide

You asked what other people have built, because you can picture the fix but not name it. The naming problem is real and it has a cause: “pipeline node panel clutter” is three different solved problems wearing one coat. Separate them and each one already has a mature reference implementation, a vocabulary, and in one case a Flutter package you could adopt this week.

01 The three-way split

Your four complaints about the node panel — clutter, scrap-vs-rejection, no save signal, no navigation on save — do not belong to one component. They belong to three, and the reason no single search turned anything up is that no single project solves all three.

Problem A

The canvas

Nodes, ports, links, pan and zoom, layout. Fully commoditised — dozens of libraries, one of them native Flutter. You already have this working; it is not your complaint.

Problem B

The inspector

The panel that edits whichever node is selected. This is your actual complaint — clutter and save state both live here. Solved thoroughly by the BPMN tooling world, and almost nowhere else.

Problem C

The node taxonomy

What kinds of node exist, and what each kind is allowed to carry. Scrap versus rejection is a taxonomy question, not a form-field question. Manufacturing execution systems have settled vocabulary for it.

02 The vocabulary you were missing

These are the terms that make the rest of the internet searchable on this topic. The right-hand column is what each one buys you in Paper specifically.

Term From What it gives you
Element template Camunda Modeler A named node kind that declares which fields it exposes and with what defaults. The panel renders the template, not every field that exists. This is the whole clutter fix.
Conditional property Camunda Modeler A field that appears only when another field has a given value. Pick “Scrap node” and the scrap group appears; pick “Rejection node” and it doesn’t.
Group · Entry · ListGroup bpmn-io/properties-panel The three structural units of an inspector. A panel is a list of declared Groups, each a list of Entries — not a hand-assembled column of widgets, which is what yours is now.
Disposition MES routing The industry’s answer to your exact question. A non-conforming unit gets a disposition of scrap, rework, or use-as-is. Your “rejection node” is rework; your “scrap node” is scrap. Three values, not two.
Operation yield MES routing Expected pass-through percentage on a step. Turns the scrap branch into a number that lives on the node and can be planned against, rather than a destination picked at reconcile time.
Rework loop MES routing A rejection route back into an earlier step, with declared entrance and exit criteria and a repeat limit. The limit is the part you don’t have — without it a part can cycle forever and be counted twice.
Boundary error event BPMN 2.0 The graph-level notation for “what leaves the happy path here”. Draws scrap and rejection as edges off a step rather than as fields buried inside it.
Sink node Dataflow graphs A terminal node that consumes and never emits. Scrap destinations are sinks; naming them so makes it obvious why they carry no output item or machine.

03 The one idea worth stealing

If you read nothing else here, read about element templates.

Your panel today asks every question of every node and then hides some of them with if (_isAssemblyStep) and if (!_isOutputNode) branches written by hand in the widget tree. Camunda hit the same wall and inverted it: a node points at a template, and the template declares its fields — type, label, default, and the condition under which it shows at all. The panel becomes a renderer.

Why this collapses two of your four complaints at once

“Is this a rejection node or a scrap node?” stops being a new dropdown you bolt on. It becomes which template this node uses — and the template already knows that a scrap node needs a destination group and a material, while a rejection node needs a repeat limit and a return step. Ask the question once, at the top, and the rest of the panel follows from the answer.

It also gives you somewhere to put the scrap-versus-rejection distinction that survives to reconcile time, which the current single untyped scrapItems list does not.

05 What to open first

In this order, and stop when you can describe what you want in your own words — that is the whole purpose of this exercise.

  1. Camunda Modeler, twenty minutes, hands on

    Model any two-step process and switch element types while watching the right-hand panel. This is the fastest route from “I can picture it” to “I can name it”.

  2. The Applications section of awesome-node-based-uis

    Fifty shipped products. Skim for screenshots. You will find three or four whose inspector is what you had in mind, and then you can point at them instead of describing them.

  3. vyuh_node_flow’s live demo, on the manufacturing example

    The only one of these that is Flutter and could become a dependency rather than an inspiration. Judge whether its canvas beats what you already have.

  4. ERPNext’s Routing, Operation and BOM doctypes

    Read only these three. They will tell you whether scrap belongs on the operation, on the BOM, or on both — a question your current model answers by accident.

One thing that needs no research

Your fourth complaint — that Save shows a toast but leaves you on the builder — is a single missing call. _deletePipeline already returns you to the pipelines screen; _saveAndNotify just never learned to. That one is not a design question and shouldn’t wait for any of the above.
